Frequency Instability

Fundamental Frequency & Jitter

In Parkinson's Disease, loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra leads to rigidity and tremor of the laryngeal adductor and abductor muscles. This causes involuntary micro-fluctuations in fundamental pitch (Jitter) and difficulty sustaining stable vocal fold tension.

  • MDVP:Fo(Hz): Average pitch of vocal fold vibration.
  • MDVP:Jitter(%) & RAP: Micro-variations between consecutive vibration periods.
  • Clinical Cutoff: Healthy vocal cords maintain Jitter below 0.60%.
Amplitude Modulation

Vocal Shimmer & Subglottic Deficit

Parkinsonian hypophonia (reduced vocal volume) stems from decreased respiratory drive and incomplete vocal fold closure. Shimmer quantifies the period-to-period variability in sound wave peak amplitude.

  • MDVP:Shimmer & Shimmer(dB): Cycle-to-cycle amplitude deviation.
  • APQ3 / APQ5 / APQ11: Moving-window amplitude perturbation quotients.
  • Clinical Sign: Elevated shimmer reflects poor glottal resistance and voice fading.
Turbulence & Breathiness

Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR)

When vocal folds fail to close completely during vibration, unmodulated turbulent air leaks through the glottis. HNR measures the ratio of periodic energy to turbulent noise energy.

  • Normal Voice: HNR > 15.0 dB (smooth, tonal sound).
  • Dysphonic PD Voice: HNR often drops below 12.0 dB due to breathiness and hoarseness.
Dynamic Vocal Complexity

RPDE & Detrended Fluctuation (DFA)

Nonlinear dynamics capture the chaotic, non-periodic behavior of diseased vocal folds that traditional Fourier analysis overlooks.

  • RPDE (Recurrence Period Density Entropy): Measures the unpredictability of the speech signal attractor.
  • DFA (Fractal Exponent): Detects self-similar turbulence across different time scales.
